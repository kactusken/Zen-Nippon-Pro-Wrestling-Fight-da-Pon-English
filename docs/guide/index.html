The Game

Zen Nippon Pro Wrestling - Fight da Pon (全日本プロレス ファイトだポン！, Zen Nihon Puro Resu Faito da Pon!) is a comedy card-and-board game by Natsume, published by Masaya for the Super Famicom on 25 June 1994. It was released only in Japan.

The All Japan Pro Wrestling roster, drawn as big-headed cartoons, walks a board game of six scenarios and a finale, solving cases like the exploding Stretch Plum and the kidnapping of President Baba. Fights are settled with cards, and you collect more cards as you go.

  • Fights run in real time. They look turn-based, and listings often call them that, but they aren't. Whoever presses first attacks, and the other side can only try to stop the move. See How a Match Works.
  • You win by pin or submission. There is no time limit, count-out or ring-out, and a match can't be drawn.
  • Every wrestler is equally strong. The game holds a stat table for each wrestler, but a coding slip means it's never used. Wrestlers differ only in the cards they can use.
  • Passwords are the only save. The cartridge has no battery. Note your password at the end of every scenario. See Passwords.

Your First Ten Minutes

  1. Press Start at the title, then choose GAME START, SCENARIO, and any of the first five squares.
  2. On the board, hold A, watch the 1-6 gauge on the left, and let go. Your wrestler walks that many spaces.
  3. Before your first match you land in Deck Edit, and your deck starts empty. Fill all five slots, because going in with no cards loses on the spot. See Deck Edit.
  4. In the ring, move along the cards with Left and Right and press A to play one. When the other wrestler starts a move, press A on as many ready cards as you can to try to reverse it.
  5. When your opponent is down, play FALL to pin. Do it while their cards are face down, or they will kick out.

How a Match Works

The card row

Each side has six cards: five moves and FALL at the far right. There's no hand to draw from and nothing runs out. Play a card and it turns face down, showing the PON back, then flips up again when it has recharged. Recharge times are in the card table; they keep running during moves and pins. Against the computer, its cards recharge about a second faster than yours.

The yellow bar by each name is health. Everyone has 255, health never comes back during a match, and every match starts full. The portrait's face changes as health drops.

Attacking and guarding

When nothing is happening, the first press attacks, and if both sides press on the same frame the player wins the tie. A move then plays out, and it can't be cancelled by the attacker.

While a move is running, any ready card you press adds its Guard to your defence. Part way through the move the game compares the two sides, and if defence is higher the move is reversed: the attacker takes a small fixed amount of damage (0 to 13) instead of dealing the move's damage. Equal doesn't count, the attack holds.

So every card is both an attack and a guard, and a card spent guarding can't attack until it recharges. Guards count only for the current move and never carry over.

The wrestlers also lock up by themselves as they circle, starting a small move with no card played. It's worth 3 to 13 damage, and it can come every few seconds if you hang back.

Going down and getting up

Some moves leave the victim down. The lower your health, the longer you stay down: about 255 minus your health in frames, plus a third of a second. Mashing does not help. The sound plays, but the press does nothing, and a card you play while down is simply wasted.

You can also attack a downed opponent. The move hauls them up first, then runs as normal.

Winning

Pinfall. Some moves pin automatically (see the Kind column in the card table), and FALL covers a downed opponent. On an opponent who's standing, FALL is wasted. The count takes about four seconds. The pinned wrestler kicks out if their defence ever passes the attack, and that defence grows on its own in proportion to their health and with any guards they press. Each kick-out adds a permanent +24 to the pinner's later pins that match.

Submission KO. Holds (Cobra Twist, Front Neck Lock, Stretch Plum) deal damage every second and a half or so. Once the victim's displayed health is 0, each tick has a 1 in 8 chance of ending the match at once, with no count.

A wrestler at 0 health keeps fighting until pinned or forced out. Health hitting zero doesn't end anything by itself.

Glowing cards

Each main wrestler has one signature card. When it finishes recharging the game may light it up gold, and it stays lit until used. A glowing card gets +80 Attack and hits a second time for 31 extra damage.

The chance depends on how many times that card has been reversed since it last glowed:

Times reversed012345+
Chance to glow1/2561/161/81/41/2Always

So having a signature card reversed is what charges it up, and this applies to the computer too. Equip your signature card and keep using it. The reversals count for that card slot during that match only.

The numbers

Attack  = 109 + Power + Random + 80 if glowing + (255 - victim's health) / 4
Defence = 104 + the Guard of every card played during this move
Reversed if Attack is lower than Defence

Attack is fixed the moment the card is pressed. With no guards every attack lands, since even the weakest attack beats 104. To stop a Power 56 card at full health you need about 61 to 74 Guard, which is two or three cards in the second or two before the check. As the victim's health falls, attacks get stronger, up to +63, so it gets harder and harder to reverse anything. There is no comeback mechanic.

Tactics

  • Watch the top row. Face-down computer cards can't guard. The computer guards by stepping along its row and pressing every 16 frames, without looking at the move, so right after it has spent its cards is your best moment.
  • Pin only when its cards are down. With its cards ready, the computer kicked out of a Power Slam pin even at 0 health. With its cards spent, it stayed down from 136 health and below.
  • The first press matters. The attacker keeps the initiative, so a short-recharge card like Jumping Shoulder (2.7 seconds) or Dropkick (3.2) gives you more first presses.
  • Keep a guard in reserve. Two high-Guard cards left ready can reverse a heavy move. Tiger Suplex has the highest Guard, 52.
  • Holds spend the computer's guards. Each tick checks for a reversal and both sides gain +2 defence, so a hold soaks up its cards, leaving it exposed afterwards. They are also the only way to win without a pin.
  • Spit and Microphone are ordinary cards. They have no special effects. Nothing in this game does: no poison, stun or status of any kind.
How the computer plays

Every opponent uses the same logic, in every chapter. The only differences between them are their decks, and all computer cards recharge 64 frames faster than yours.

Every 8 frames in the open, it moves its cursor one step toward a target card and, once there, may press. Which cards it aims for depends on a level from 0 to 3 that follows the total Power of the attacks you have made, wrapping around every 64 points of Power, so it wanders through the match: level 0 uses slots 1 to 3, levels 1 and 2 mostly slots 3 to 5. Level 3, or any time you are down, uses FALL 9 times out of 16. It really does go for the pin when you're on the mat.

Its chance of pressing depends on your health in 32-point bands, which looks like a slip in the original. Its press rate jumps up and down as you lose health rather than climbing.

The Board

Walking

There are no dice. Hold A and the gauge moves, changing direction at random; let go and you walk that many spaces, from 1 to 6. You can aim, but the gauge turns unpredictably. It favours the middle: in testing it spent about two thirds of its time on 2, 3 and 4.

Paths are one-way, so you can't go back. At a fork the walker stops and you pick a direction with the D-pad. A story stop or the goal always stops you, even with steps left. Other spaces only take effect if you end your walk there.

Deck Edit

Deck Edit opens before every story fight. Choose a slot with A, then a card. FALL is always your sixth card, so you only fill the five move slots.

  • A new game gives you eight cards: Brainbuster, Piledriver, Backdrop, Lariat, Elbow, Power Slam, Cobra Twist and German Suplex.
  • Your deck starts empty. An empty deck loses at the bell with GIVE UP!
  • Each wrestler can equip only some cards. Those eight, plus DDT and Powerbomb, work for all 16 main wrestlers. A forbidden card just buzzes. See Wrestlers.
  • A card can sit in only one deck at a time, so in team fights your cards are shared out across the team.

Getting cards

Fixed card spaces are the sure way to get the wrestler-specific moves; each map lists them. Random cards are drawn from all 42, and until you own more than 16 you always get a new one. After that repeats are possible (You already have this card). Once all six scenarios are cleared, random cards come only from the first 16.

Losing a story fight costs nothing. You flip a free random card and can fight again, a point one of the villains complains about. Only the random Match spaces can cost you a card, through Card trouble.

Passwords

The game shows a 16-character password after you clear each scenario. It stores which cards you own and which scenarios you've cleared. It doesn't store your deck, so you rebuild that afterwards. To enter one, choose PASSWORD at the title. On the password screen, pick letters with the D-pad and A; BS deletes, the arrows move along the line, and END confirms.

The game's passwords only use A to Z and 0 to 5. Digits 6 to 9 are on the grid too, but they count as A to D.

Chapters

The first five scenarios are open from the start, in any order. The sixth stays locked behind a red X until they are all cleared, and clearing it takes you straight on to the finale. A cleared square changes colour and can't be picked again.

This guide numbers them 1 to 6 by their place on the Scenario Select screen. The story never branches: the only choices are forks on the board and the wrestlers you pick, and there is one ending. Chapter 1 is the sensible start: it's the shortest board, with the weakest Grunts.

On the maps, each space is drawn in its position on the board. Start and goal are labelled, forks have a ring, and lettered spaces are the stops listed in the chapter's spoiler box. Hover a space to see what it is. Matches on a board are always against the Grunts named in that chapter.

The 16 main wrestlers, all selectable in Battle. Debug menu

The developers' menu is still in the game, switched off by a single byte. The separate (Debug Menu) patch turns it on: hold X and press Select on any screen. It has a chapter select (EVENT_01 to 07), the deck, wrestler and stage select screens, a scene viewer and a script tester. Nothing leaves most rows, so use the reset, Start plus Select, to get out. It's unfinished developer tooling, and some rows show garbled graphics.

Zebra Mask

Spoiler: the wrestler you can't fight

Zebra Mask is nearly a complete wrestler, with a full set of ring animations, a portrait and a name bar, but nothing in the game ever makes this wrestler your opponent. There is no deck or real name in the data. Forced in with an emulator's debugger, Zebra Mask fought a full match.

Q&A

Is there an English version?

Not from the publisher: the game was only released in Japan. This guide was written alongside an English fan translation, released as a patch for your own copy of the game.

Is it turn-based?

No. Both sides act in real time, and whoever presses first attacks. See Attacking and guarding.

Who's the best wrestler?

Nobody, in raw numbers: all wrestlers share the same Attack and Guard. What differs is the cards. In Battle, a look at the decks is the whole comparison.

What happens if I lose?

In a story fight you get a free card and can try again. On a board Match space you go to Card trouble and may lose a card. See Getting cards.

Why did my match end at once with GIVE UP?

Your deck was empty. Equip at least one move in Deck Edit; it won't do it for you.

Why does the computer always kick out?

Its cards were ready. Pin right after it has spent them. See Tactics.

Can I miss a fixed card?

Yes: a fixed card only counts if you end your walk on it, and paths are one-way. You can't go back during the chapter, and a cleared chapter can't be picked again, though a password from before you cleared it reopens it. Random cards can still give you the card.
